# Wear Your Batik pitching script

Working format: three minutes including approximately 45 seconds of screen actions. The organiser's live pitch duration and whether Q&A is included remain unconfirmed; the two-minute requirement applies to the submission video. Timing below is estimated, not rehearsed.

## Spoken script and cues

### 0:00–0:25 — Concept

“When you see a beautiful batik pattern, you might admire it. But what if you could make it personal and imagine wearing it?

We're Haziq and Timothy, and this is Wear Your Batik: a browser experience that takes you from choosing a motif to creating your own cloth and exploring it on a garment.”

### 0:25–0:50 — Culture × Tech

“Our inspiration is batik from Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia. We want to make that inspiration approachable for someone who has never designed textiles.

The cultural idea is participation. Instead of only looking at finished patterns, visitors experiment with colour, repetition and their own marks. These are contemporary batik-inspired designs, with artisan collaboration as our next step.”

### 0:50–1:40 — Demonstrate the journey

[Show the app. Select Patterns, choose one motif, then change its palette.]

“Let's make one. I choose a botanical motif, change the colours, and the cloth and garment update together.”

[Add one stamp or short mark. Show its repeat.]

“Now I add my own mark. That small choice becomes part of the repeating design.”

[Show garment placement, then Preview. Rotate only if 3D is working on the presentation device; otherwise show the flat preview.]

“Then I explore how the pattern sits on a garment.”

[Switch explicitly to the recorded synthetic-person example, displaying its matching source shirt beside the result.]

“This recorded example shows our AI try-on with a synthetic person. It helps you imagine wearing the design. The exact source stays visible because AI details can vary, and fit is not guaranteed.”

### 1:40–2:15 — Best Use of Tech

“Codex was central to building the experience. It helped translate our brief into the creative workflow, connect the pattern renderer to garment previews and the image service, and test the implementation.

The interesting use of technology is the bridge between a design you control precisely and an appearance you can imagine. We deliberately show both, so the AI result doesn't replace your original creation.”

### 2:15–2:40 — Execution and team

“We built and deployed a working prototype. All 25 automated tests and the production build pass.

I directed the concept and development. Timothy helped with ideation and testing. Our next priorities are artisan feedback, real-device testing and improving how faithfully the wearing preview follows the pattern.”

### 2:40–3:00 — Close

[Return to a strong cloth-and-garment view. Pause after the final line.]

“Our goal is simple: give someone a first step into making something personal with batik inspiration.

Wear Your Batik. From appreciation, to participation, to a look you can imagine wearing.”

## Demo preparation and recovery

- Use one adult shirt for the demonstration. Have its selected motif and palette ready before speaking.
- Keep the recorded synthetic example in a separate tab or slide with its matching source garment: artifacts/tryon/leaf/shirt.png and artifacts/tryon/leaf/result.png. Do not imply it is the result of the newly edited design.
- If live AI generation is used, request it before the pitch or allow a clearly labelled time cut. Do not spend the pitch waiting for a response.
- Test 3D on the presentation laptop first. The final review browser displayed the 2D fallback; use the flat preview if needed and omit rotation claims.
- If the app stalls, say: “I'll show the recorded example so you can see the completed interaction.” Then switch immediately.
- Avoid claims of cultural authenticity, artisan endorsement, accurate fit or production-ready try-on fidelity. None has been established.
- Rehearse with a timer. If over three minutes, cut the test-count sentence and shorten the team section; keep the cultural explanation and completed visual outcome.

## If the live slot is only two minutes

Use the existing submission-video script, or compress this pitch to: 15 seconds concept, 20 seconds cultural interpretation, 45 seconds demonstration, 25 seconds Codex and team, 15 seconds close. Do not infer the live presentation limit from the video requirement.
